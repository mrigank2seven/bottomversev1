02 / GET THE BASICS RIGHT

How to Measure Yourself.

Accurate measurements are the foundation of a good fit. A soft tape and five calm minutes are all you need.

03 / SMALL DETAILS, BETTER RESULTS

Get More Accurate
Measurements.

  1. Use a soft measuring tape.
  2. Measure over light clothing or directly on the body.
  3. Keep the tape level.
  4. Don't pull the tape too tight.
  5. Stand naturally.
  6. Take each measurement twice.
DO

Keep it level

A level tape gives you a reliable number.

DON'T

Pull or twist

Tension and a tilted tape distort the result.

04 / SIZE IS NOT THE SAME AS FIT

Know Your Fit.

A 40-inch chest can wear different sizes depending on the garment cut and the silhouette you want. Select a fit to compare.

07 / THE IMPORTANT BIT

Body Measurement
≠ Garment Measurement.

Your body measurement is the measurement of you. Garment measurement is the measurement of the actual clothing — it is normally larger to create ease, movement, comfort and the intended fit.

08 / DECISION HELPER

Between Two
Sizes?

Answer three quick questions for a sensible tie-breaker.

1. How do you prefer your clothes to fit?
2. What fabric are you buying?
3. What type of garment?

If you're between M and L and prefer a balanced fit, begin with the size chart — choose L if your chest or shoulder is near the upper limit.

11 / FOR STRUCTURED PIECES

Buying Tailored
Clothing?

Tailored garments may need more precise numbers: chest, shoulder, waist, sleeve, neck, jacket length, trouser waist, hip, thigh, knee and inseam.

“Tailored fit does not mean tight. It means the garment follows the shape of your body more closely while maintaining movement and proportion.”

12 / AVOID THESE

Common Fit Mistakes.

01

Buying based only on height

Height says little about chest, shoulder or waist proportions.

02

Assuming every brand fits the same

Sizing and garment measurements change from brand to brand.

03

Ignoring shoulder fit

The shoulder is particularly important on shirts and jackets.

04

Measuring over thick clothing

Bulky layers add numbers that don't belong to your body.

05

Choosing only by letter size

An M in one brand does not guarantee an M in another.

06

Confusing slim with small

Slim is the garment's cut — it doesn't mean automatically size down.
